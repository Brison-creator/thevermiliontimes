SpaceX did it.

At about **3:20 p.m. Central on Saturday**, the upper stage of its Starship rocket, Ship 42, fell back to Starbase, Texas, after a day in orbit and settled into the giant steel arms of the same tower it launched from. It is the first time any rocket company has caught a spaceship returning from orbit.

"That was the holy grail of space flight, plummeting through fire to be snatched out of the air by steel," SpaceX's Dan Huot said on the company's webcast, as reported by Space.com. "We caught a Starship, and now everything changes."

## A day around the world

Ship 42 lifted off Friday at about 4:47 p.m. Central. It reached orbit, released 26 Starlink internet satellites and circled Earth 14 times over 22 and a half hours, the longest Starship flight yet.

On Saturday afternoon flight controllers gave the final go to bring it home. It fired one engine for about 10 seconds to drop out of orbit, then plunged through the atmosphere wrapped in glowing plasma, flipped upright at the last moment and lowered itself into the tower's "chopsticks."

## The booster missed its catch

The other half of the rocket didn't make it back. On Friday, the Super Heavy booster turned away from the tower and came down offshore in the Gulf.

SpaceX's Kate Tice said Saturday that early signs point to the booster cutting its return burn short because of a "low fuel mass alarm." Its guidance then saw it was out of position and correctly called off the catch, she said, and SpaceX is still looking for the root cause. SpaceX has caught Super Heavy boosters three times before.

## Why this matters in Vermilion Parish

This is the rocket SpaceX plans to launch from the coast at Pecan Island, a site its job listings call a "Starship high-rate launch site." Flying that often depends on catching both stages and flying them again within days. Until Saturday, nobody had shown the top half could come home from orbit in one piece.

The state says building at the Louisiana site is set to start in 2027, with launches planned from 2029. SpaceX has also told the FAA it has not yet applied for Starship launches from Louisiana. [Read what we know about Starbase Louisiana.](/news/starbase-louisiana-what-we-know/)
