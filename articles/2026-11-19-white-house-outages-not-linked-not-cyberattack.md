A nationwide ground stop. Bank apps that wouldn't load. Apple's App Store going dark. After a week of failures, a lot of people have been asking the same question: is somebody doing this?

The White House says no.

In a release Wednesday, the administration said it sees no sign of a coordinated cyberattack, a foreign government or any other bad actor behind the recent outages. It quoted the two officials who have been answering for it: Director of National Intelligence **Jay Clayton** and **Marco Rubio**, the secretary of state and national security adviser.

"This is not a cyberattack, and this is not a coordinated effort by a nation state," Clayton said.

## Not connected, they say

Rubio said the incidents have nothing to do with one another: "the Microsoft incident has nothing to do with what we saw with the FAA. The FAA has nothing to do with what we saw with the banks a few days ago."

"We are not under systemic attack," he said.

## So what is it?

Clayton's explanation is that a great many companies and agencies are fixing security holes and replacing old equipment at the same moment, and some of that work is going badly.

Systems are being taken down, patched and upgraded, he said, and "they're not coming back as expected in all cases." Trouble also shows up where one system has to hand work off to another.

"Our systems need upgrades. They need patching," Clayton said.
