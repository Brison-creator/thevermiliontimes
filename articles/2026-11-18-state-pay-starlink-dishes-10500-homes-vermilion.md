Some of the hardest-to-reach homes in Louisiana are getting satellite internet with the dish paid for by the state, and a couple hundred of them are in Vermilion Parish.

## The agreement

Louisiana signed a grant agreement with SpaceX's Starlink on June 11 under the federal Broadband Equity, Access and Deployment program, known as BEAD, according to ConnectLA, the state broadband office. The office said the deal covers **10,635 locations in more than 50 parishes**.

Starlink is one piece of a much larger plan. The state has $1.355 billion in BEAD money and is spreading it across fiber, fixed wireless and satellite providers, with a goal of reaching every home and business by 2028.

## Who pays for the dish

ConnectLA Executive Director **Veneeth Iyengar** told KLFY this week that the state, and not the resident, will pay for the hardware.

"We will pay for that piece of equipment, not the resident, once SpaceX is ready to roll out," Iyengar told the station. KLFY reported that eligible households would pay $55 a month for service.

Iyengar also told KLFY that Vermilion Parish is on the list: "even in the island area, there are a couple of hundred locations" where the state is paying for a Starlink unit.

Why satellite there and fiber somewhere else? Iyengar said it comes down to which companies bid on which addresses.

## A new round is opening

ConnectLA's website says the state is **opening a new round of grants** and has posted an updated list of locations. The public has until **Jan. 2** to review the list and weigh in. Iyengar told KLFY the office expects to announce the results of that round in February.

The location map and the updated list are on ConnectLA's program page at connect.la.gov/bead.

## If you live down the bayou

The Times has not seen the list of Vermilion addresses or a rollout date for the parish. If you've been told your home is one of them, or you've been waiting years for a decent connection, tell us at news@thevermiliontimes.com.
