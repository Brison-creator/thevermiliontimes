Three Vermilion Parish residents and a newly formed citizens' group have gone to court to undo the parish's tax agreement with SpaceX, arguing that public officials approved it out of public view.

Filed Oct. 26 in the 15th Judicial District Court, the case targets two bodies: the Police Jury and the parish's Industrial Development Board, WWNO and the Gulf States Newsroom reported. Bringing the case are the Vermilion Citizens Coalition and residents Cheryl Choate of Pecan Island, Rebecca C. Young and Dr. Robert J. Young III.

## The complaint

The residents count eight breaches of Louisiana's Open Meetings Law. The ones described in the reporting include:

- **No livestream.** The Police Jury's July 15 and Aug. 19 meetings were not streamed online, as the suit says the law requires.
- **Thin notice.** Notices for the development board's July 23 and Aug. 25 meetings were tacked to the courthouse door instead of going online or to the state commissioner of administration.
- **A code name.** The Aug. 25 agenda called the deal "Project Osprey" rather than describing it.
- **A mid-meeting revival.** The Police Jury revived the dormant development board on July 15 by changing its agenda during the meeting, which the plaintiffs call a "subterfuge."

## What they want

The plaintiffs ask a judge to throw out what the development board did on Aug. 25, when it approved the payment-in-lieu-of-taxes deal, so the vote would have to happen again in the open.

## The money in play

Under the deal, SpaceX's yearly payments to the parish begin at $25 million and grow 2% annually over a 25-year term. The Gulf States Newsroom totals that at about $800 million, against a property tax break it values at close to $23.8 billion over the same stretch.

## The responses

Plaintiffs' attorney William Most of New Orleans said the law exists to "make sure that our public officials are working for us, not the other way around," WWNO reported.

The Police Jury declined to comment and the development board did not respond, according to the station. The board's own minutes state the Aug. 25 notice was "duly posted and given in the manner provided by the Open Meetings Law." The governor's office said its executive counsel was present on July 15 and that the settlement was discussed in closed session before a public vote. SpaceX did not respond.

No court date had been reported as of Friday.

## Nearby: school land

In a separate development, Acadiana News First reported Oct. 28 that the parish school district holds roughly 4,000 acres of Section 16 education land beside the planned spaceport near White Lake. That kind of land can be traded but not sold without the Legislature's approval, and an appraisal would come before any talks. "Basically, we have to look at that land," Superintendent Tommy Byler told the station.
