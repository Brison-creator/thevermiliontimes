The second half of SpaceX's big test comes this afternoon.

Starship's upper stage, **Ship 42**, has spent the night circling Earth and is in good health, SpaceX said Saturday morning. If flight controllers give the final go, it will fall back through the atmosphere and try to land in the arms of the launch tower at Starbase, Texas, at about **3:16 to 3:20 p.m. Central**, according to SpaceX's updates on its webcast, as reported by Space.com.

No rocket company has ever caught an orbital spaceship this way.

## Heat shield looks good

Engineers inspected the ship's tiles two ways: with long-range cameras on the ground, and with cameras riding on a few of the Starlink satellites the ship let go on Friday.

The heat shield team signed off after reviewing those images, SpaceX spokesperson Dan Huot said on the webcast, saying it is "in a good posture" to come back through the atmosphere and home to the launch site.

Overnight, flight controllers chose to skip an early chance to splash down off South America and keep the ship in space for the full flight. "We are one step closer to an eventual return to launch site," Huot said.

## What happened to the booster

Friday's launch went up on time at about 4:47 p.m. Central, but the Super Heavy booster didn't get caught. It headed for the tower, then turned away and came down offshore in the Gulf.

On Saturday, Huot said the booster's guidance system worked as designed: it "detected that the booster was not in the place that it was supposed to be to proceed with a catch, did an automated abort" and headed offshore. SpaceX is still looking for the root cause. [Read Friday's launch story.](/news/starship-flight-15-launch-today-double-catch/)

## Why Pecan Island is watching

This is the rocket SpaceX plans to fly from the coast at Pecan Island, a site the company calls a "Starship high-rate launch site." Flying that often only works if the ships come home in one piece. A catch today would be the first proof it can be done from orbit.

SpaceX streams the landing on its website and X account. A final go/no-go call comes shortly before the landing. We'll update this story with the result.
