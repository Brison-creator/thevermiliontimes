Fewer people were stopped at America's borders in October than in any October CBP has on record.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection said Monday that it logged **27,890 encounters nationwide** last month, the lowest number it has ever recorded to open a federal fiscal year, which starts Oct. 1. That was 7% below September and the fewest for any month since February, according to the agency.

## By the numbers

- **8,288:** Border Patrol apprehensions along the southwest border in October, down 7% from September. CBP says that is 95% below the monthly average under the Biden administration and fewer than the agency recorded in five days in October 2024.
- **18 months:** how long CBP says it has gone with zero releases of migrants at the border.
- **44%:** the jump in combined drug seizures from September, by weight. Agents seized about 14,700 pounds of methamphetamine and more than 9,000 pounds of cocaine in October.
- **$391 billion:** imports CBP processed in October, with **$26 billion** in duties identified for collection.

"President Trump unleashed the full strength of DHS to secure the border and enforce the law," Homeland Security Secretary **Markwayne Mullin** said in the release. "The results speak for themselves."

CBP Commissioner **Rodney S. Scott** said the agency now knows "more about who and what is entering our homeland than at any time in our nation's history."

## What "encounters" means

An encounter counts each time CBP stops someone: Border Patrol arrests between ports of entry, plus people turned away as inadmissible at ports of entry. The same person can be counted more than once. The agency's full monthly numbers are posted on its public stats pages.
