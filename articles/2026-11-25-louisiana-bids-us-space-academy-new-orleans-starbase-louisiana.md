Louisiana is trying to land a school that does not exist yet: a national academy for the country's future astronauts and rocket builders.

The state has formally asked NASA to build the United States Space Academy in New Orleans, on the lakefront campus of LSU New Orleans. Part of its argument is what is planned for Vermilion Parish.

"The United States Space Academy belongs in a state that builds the rockets, supports our nation's military and is now home to the world's largest spaceport," Gov. Jeff Landry said in the state's announcement.

That spaceport is SpaceX's Starbase Louisiana at Pecan Island. It has not been built. SpaceX says construction starts in 2027.

## What the academy would be

The state describes it as a proposed federal academy, backed by NASA, that would train the people who will fly, build and run the country's space programs.

President Donald Trump set up a commission on the academy in August, the state says. NASA then invited every governor to submit a proposal.

## What Louisiana is offering

- **Where:** A "campus within a campus" at LSU New Orleans, with its own dorm, classrooms and parade ground.
- **How many:** 300 cadets in the fall of 2028, growing to 1,200 by 2031 and 4,000 or more over time.
- **How soon:** The state says the campus would be ready by the third quarter of 2027.
- **Money:** A new state fund to cover startup costs, which the state says would lower what Washington has to spend.
- **Backers:** Over 100 companies, schools and other groups, plus every member of Louisiana's delegation in Congress, according to the state.

## The case it is making

The state's pitch is that the rockets are already built here. NASA's Michoud Assembly Facility in New Orleans, about nine miles from the campus, built the first stages of the Saturn V moon rockets, the space shuttle's fuel tanks and the core stages of today's Artemis rockets.

Cadets would learn alongside people working at Michoud, at NASA's Stennis Space Center in Mississippi and at "SpaceX Starbase Louisiana," the proposal says.

## What is not known

- **Who is competing.** Every state was invited. The announcement does not say how many applied.
- **When NASA will decide.** The state did not give a date.
- **What it would cost.** The announcement gives no dollar figure for the state fund or the campus work.

For Vermilion Parish, the proposal is one more sign of how much the state is counting on the Pecan Island project. SpaceX is [taking resumes for it now](/news/spacex-taking-resumes-starbase-louisiana-email-supplier-signup/).
