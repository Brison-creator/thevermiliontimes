Friday's nationwide flight shutdown started a lot closer to home than most people think.

The Federal Aviation Administration says one of the two places it traced the trouble to was an FAA **radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas**, where the agency's network contractor, L3Harris, had been installing a **new router** that day. El Dorado sits just north of the Louisiana line, about **225 miles north of Abbeville** as the crow flies, or roughly a five-hour drive.

The other site the FAA named was a radar facility in **Amarillo, Texas**.

## What happened

At about **2:30 p.m. Central** on Friday, Nov. 13, the FAA's older equipment became unstable as its telecommunications network was swamped, the agency said, with **a thousand connection requests landing every few seconds.** Radio and radar feeds started to falter at **17 of the FAA's 22 air route traffic control centers**, the big facilities that guide planes between airports, and the problem spread to several approach control facilities.

The FAA ordered a **nationwide ground stop**. It says about **3,500 planes** already in the air landed safely, and that some radio frequencies and radar feeds kept working the whole time. L3Harris blocked the two sites, the network settled down, and flights resumed at about **7:15 p.m. Central.**

Airlines canceled about **12,462 flights** that day, according to the FAA, though not all of them were caused by the ground stop.

## Old copper, new fiber

Transportation Secretary **Sean Duffy** said on CBS's "Face the Nation" Sunday that the telecom provider for the El Dorado site was retiring an old copper line and forced the switch to fiber. "We brought in our routers and did the work, and that's when we saw the flooding of the network," he said.

Duffy said that kind of switch has been done "thousands of times" without a problem, which is why crews at a remote site like El Dorado did it during daytime hours. "We're going to have to reevaluate now when we do this work," he said.

The FAA says it has **no evidence of a cyberattack**. As a precaution, it turned off the same function on about **60 similar routers** across the system. The agency and L3Harris are still looking for the root cause, with the FBI offering support, Duffy said.

## Why it matters here

Duffy said Sunday that flights should be back to full capacity by Monday. He is also asking Congress for **$50 billion** to rebuild the air traffic control network, which he called a 1980s system still running on copper wire.
