Louisiana's senior senator went on national television Sunday and didn't hold back on Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

Sen. **Bill Cassidy**, a Republican and a physician who chairs the Senate health committee, told CBS's "Face the Nation" that Kennedy is "obviously trying to provide cover for failure" at the Department of Health and Human Services.

Host Margaret Brennan opened the interview by citing a KFF report that measles is officially no longer considered eliminated in the United States.

## "You could be the person that brings death to another"

Cassidy's message to parents was blunt. Infants can't be vaccinated against measles until around their first birthday, so they rely on everyone around them having had the shot.

"If you've not been vaccinated, you could be the person that brings death to another," he said. "We're seeing children die from measles who are too young to be vaccinated."

He said that message should be "pounded, pounded, pounded," and that if he were president, he would pick a new health secretary. He listed rising health costs, measles deaths, the lost elimination status and little progress on ultra-processed foods.

Brennan played a clip of Kennedy saying "the Senate no longer has any leverage over me" now that Democrats will control the chamber. Cassidy called that "a series of failures" being covered up.

## Undecided on the FDA pick

Cassidy said he still doesn't know how his committee will vote **Wednesday** on Dr. Heidi Overton, President Trump's nominee for FDA commissioner. "I've been working the phones all week," he said. "I cannot tell you the outcome of that vote right now."

## Social Security stalled

Cassidy also said he has commitments from Senate leaders and the president to move a bipartisan Social Security bill, but he blamed Sen. Ron Wyden of Oregon, the top Democrat on the Senate Finance Committee, for refusing to take part. His own plan would borrow $1.5 trillion and invest it in a fund separate from the Social Security trust fund to grow over decades.

"This is so important to our country," Cassidy said. "Congress is there to take tough choices, and now those tough choices are being ducked."
