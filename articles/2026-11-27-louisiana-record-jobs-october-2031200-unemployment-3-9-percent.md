More people are on a payroll in Louisiana than at any time on record.

Employers in the state had **2,031,200 jobs** in October, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. That is the highest count in federal numbers that go back to 1990. The October figure is an early estimate and can be revised.

## How fast it is growing

- **Up 3,900 jobs** from September.
- **Up 40,000 jobs** from October 2025, a gain of 2%.
- **Thirteen months in a row.** The count has risen every month since September 2025.

For most of the last decade, Louisiana could not get back to its old high of 2,007,200 jobs, set in December 2014. It finally passed that mark in April and has kept climbing.

## Unemployment

The state's unemployment rate fell to **3.9%** in October. It was 4.5% in May. The last time it was this low was October 2023.

The national rate in October was 4.1%.

One caution: the rate counts only people who are working or looking for work. That group, called the labor force, has shrunk by about 6,300 people in Louisiana since August. A smaller labor force can pull the rate down even without new hiring.

## Closer to home

- **Vermilion Parish:** The parish unemployment rate was 3.5% in September, the latest month the government has counted. A year earlier it was 4.1%. It is the lowest September rate here since 2022. Parish numbers are not adjusted for the seasons, so they bounce around more than the state's.
- **Lafayette area:** The Lafayette metro area, which includes Vermilion Parish, had 190,700 jobs in October. That is 2,400 more than a year earlier and just under September's record of 190,900.

## Where the jobs are

Construction is a big part of it. As the Times [reported Nov. 22](/news/louisiana-adding-construction-jobs-faster-than-any-state/), Louisiana had 153,400 construction jobs in October, about 17,600 more than a year before.
