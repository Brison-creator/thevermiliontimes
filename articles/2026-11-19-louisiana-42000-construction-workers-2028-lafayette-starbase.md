The plants, data centers and launch pads Louisiana has been celebrating still have to be built, and a new report says the state doesn't have nearly enough people to build them.

Leaders for a Better Louisiana, a statewide business and policy group, looked at 12 big industrial and technology projects announced over roughly the past year. Together they add up to about **$282 billion** in investment and about **7,550 permanent jobs**, the group says.

Building them is another matter. At the peak, in **mid-2028**, the 12 projects alone could need **nearly 42,000 construction workers**, which the group says is about half of all the industrial construction workers Louisiana has today.

## Why it matters here

**SpaceX's Starbase Louisiana**, planned for the coast of Vermilion Parish, is one of the 12 projects, according to the project list published by 10/12 Industry Report. The others include LNG export plants near Lake Charles, Meta's data center in north Louisiana and the Hyundai steel mill.

Better Louisiana says the squeeze will be worst in three regions: **Lafayette, Lake Charles and Monroe**. Together they make up **81%** of the work at its peak, yet just **17%** of Louisiana's industrial construction workers live there. Put every one of those local workers on these jobs, the group figures, and the three regions still come up **almost 19,000 people short**.

## It's the skills, too

The group says the gap is not only a head count. Data centers need heating and cooling workers, LNG plants lean on pipefitters, and nearly every project needs electricians. Louisiana has stepped up training, it says, but there are still gaps in several high-skill trades.

## Not a short boom

The peak comes in the next year or so, but the work doesn't stop there. Better Louisiana estimates the 12 projects will keep **upward of 20,000** construction workers on the job for the next decade or longer. 10/12 Industry Report, citing the full report, says that long tail is driven largely by Starbase Louisiana and runs through early 2041.

The group's chief executive, Adam Knapp, said in a statement reported by 10/12 Industry Report that "Louisiana has an extraordinary opportunity ahead," and that the state needs more skilled workers and closer ties between employers and training programs.
