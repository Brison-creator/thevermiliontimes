The natural gas that heats homes and runs power plants across the country costs about a fifth less than it did a year ago.

Gas sold for **$3.30** per million British thermal units on Tuesday at the Henry Hub, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. On the same date last year it was $4.15.

The Henry Hub is in Erath. It is a spot where big pipelines meet, and the federal government describes it as the delivery point for the natural gas contract traded in New York. When a news report gives "the price of natural gas," it is usually giving the price at this hub in Vermilion Parish.

## The numbers

- **This week:** $3.30 on Nov. 24.
- **A year ago:** $4.15 on Nov. 24, 2025.
- **This month so far:** an average of $2.99. Last November averaged $3.79.
- **Low for the year:** $2.42 on Oct. 30.
- **High for the year:** $30.72 on Jan. 23.

## That January spike

The January number is not a typo. The price went from $4 on Jan. 20 to $30.72 three days later, then dropped back to about $4 by early February.

It is the highest daily price in federal records that go back to 1997.

## Why it matters

- **Heating and power bills.** Gas is the fuel for many furnaces, water heaters and power plants, so the hub price is one of the things that feeds into bills.
- **The other side of it.** Louisiana produces natural gas. A lower price means less money for the companies that drill for it and for the people who own the mineral rights.

Winter is when the most gas gets burned for heat, and January showed how fast the price can move.

The Times will keep an eye on the Henry Hub price through the winter.
