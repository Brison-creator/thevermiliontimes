Prices are still rising faster than most families would like. In October, incomes rose faster.

The federal government's broadest price measure was **3.8% higher** in October than a year earlier, the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis reported Wednesday. In September it was 3.7%, by the Times' math from the bureau's numbers. The Federal Reserve aims for 2%.

Leaving out food and energy, which jump around the most, prices were up 3.3% from a year ago.

## Paychecks

- **Personal income rose 0.7%** in October, or $201.8 billion across the country.
- **After taxes and inflation, income rose 0.5%.** It had slipped 0.2% in September.
- **Wages and salaries rose about 0.4%** for the month and are up about 4.1% from a year ago, just ahead of prices.

Part of October's bump will not repeat. The bureau said government benefits jumped mostly because of one-time payments tied to a Medicare premium rebate.

## Spending

People spent $63.8 billion more in October than in September, a 0.3% increase. But prices rose 0.3% too. Take out the price increases and spending was flat. People paid more and took home about the same amount of stuff.

Most of the extra money, $52.1 billion of it, went to services instead of goods.

## Saving

Americans saved 4.1% of their after-tax income in October, up from 3.6% in September.

## What it means

The report is one of the main things the Federal Reserve watches when it sets interest rates. Inflation near 4% is about twice the Fed's goal.

The next report, covering November, comes out Dec. 23.
