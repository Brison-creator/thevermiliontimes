President Donald Trump on Friday signed a law that ends penny production and sets a national rule for what happens at the register when nobody has one.

The law is called the Common Cents Act. The White House announced the signing Friday.

## What it means at the register

The rounding rule applies **only to cash**, and only when exact change can't be given. A business is allowed to round. It is not required to.

The rounding is done on the **total, with tax included**, not on each item. It can also be done on the change you are owed.

- **Ends in 1 or 2 cents:** may be rounded down. $10.02 becomes $10.00.
- **Ends in 3 or 4 cents:** may be rounded up. $10.03 becomes $10.05.
- **Ends in 6 or 7 cents:** may be rounded down. $10.07 becomes $10.05.
- **Ends in 8 or 9 cents:** may be rounded up. $10.08 becomes $10.10.

One exception: a whole purchase of 1 or 2 cents may be rounded up to a nickel.

A business can also round in your favor any time it wants, the law says.

## What does not change

- **Pennies are still legal money.** The law says every penny already made stays legal tender. Stores can keep taking them and giving them.
- **Cards, checks, gift cards, money orders and electronic payments are not rounded.** Those are still charged to the cent.
- **Paychecks.** If an employer pays a worker in cash and chooses to round, it has to round **up**. The law says it does not excuse anyone from minimum wage or overtime rules.

## The rest of the law

- **No more pennies for circulation.** The Treasury must stop making them for everyday use. It may still make them to sell to collectors.
- **A cheaper nickel.** The Treasury may make the nickel with a zinc core and a nickel outer layer, if testing shows that saves money and works in coin machines.
- **State and local rules.** A business that follows the federal rounding rule cannot be found in violation of a state or local law for doing so.
- **Reports.** The Federal Reserve has 90 days to publish a plan for taking penny orders and deposits at the coin terminals that serve its banks. The Treasury must report on how the change affects low-income communities, older people and people without bank accounts.
- **Other coins.** Before the Treasury stops making any other coin, it must give Congress 60 days' notice and a plan.
