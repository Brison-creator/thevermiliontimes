The first summit ever held among the leaders of the United States, Russia and China ended Thursday with a one-page statement and four promises.

President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping met in Shenzhen, China, and put their names to what the White House calls a "Declaration toward Peace." The White House posted the text the same day. Here is what it says.

## 1. Talks on nuclear risk

The three leaders restated a line that American and Soviet leaders first used in the 1980s: that a nuclear war "cannot be won and must never be fought." They said their countries will hold talks on lowering nuclear risks and on keeping nuclear weapons from spreading.

The declaration doesn't say when those talks start, who will lead them or whether they would cover limits on the three countries' own arsenals.

## 2. More fuel

The three countries "plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products," the declaration says, and they stressed the need for steady energy flows. Refined products are the fuels made from crude oil, such as gasoline, diesel and jet fuel.

There are no numbers or dates attached.

## 3. Changing the United Nations

All three hold permanent seats on the U.N. Security Council. They committed to reforming the U.N. so it can respond better to world problems and stick to its core job of keeping the peace. The text doesn't describe what would change.

## 4. A message to international courts

The leaders said the consent of nations and the legal immunity of government officials are "of paramount importance," and agreed that the International Criminal Court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction."

## What it isn't

The declaration is a statement of intent, not a treaty. It names no war, sets no deadlines and creates no new organization. It says the leaders had an in-depth exchange on the state of the world and committed to "strategic stability and international security."
