Louisiana has more jobs than it has ever had. For the first time in a long time, the oilfield is not the reason.

The state added about 40,000 jobs between October 2025 and October 2026, as the Times [reported Friday](/news/louisiana-record-jobs-october-2031200-unemployment-3-9-percent/). We went through the federal numbers industry by industry to see where they came from.

## Where the new jobs are

Change from October 2025 to October 2026:

- **Construction: up 17,600.** That is 44% of all the new jobs in the state.
- **Health care and education: up 9,200**, to 360,600. That is the most on record.
- **Professional and business services: up 8,700**, to 230,200. Also a record. This group covers engineers, accountants, office staff and temp workers.
- **Restaurants, hotels and entertainment: up 5,600.**
- **Manufacturing: up 2,100.**
- **Government: up 1,700.**

## Where jobs were lost

- **Banking, insurance and real estate: down 1,400.**
- **Stores, trucking and utilities: down 1,200.**
- **Oilfield service companies: down 1,000.**

## The oilfield

This is the part that will sound familiar in Vermilion Parish.

Companies that do support work for oil and gas, the service side of the oilfield, had **19,700 jobs** in Louisiana in October. A year earlier it was 20,700. In February 2012 it was 44,800.

That is a loss of more than half the jobs in less than 15 years.

Oil and gas producers themselves had 4,900 jobs in October, close to the lowest count in records that go back to 2004. In early 2012 they had 9,500.

## What it means

The work has moved. There are now almost eight construction jobs in Louisiana for every oilfield service job. Construction added 17,600 jobs in one year. Oilfield service has lost 7,300 since October 2019.

The October numbers are early estimates and can be revised. The oilfield numbers are not adjusted for the seasons, so we compared October to October.
