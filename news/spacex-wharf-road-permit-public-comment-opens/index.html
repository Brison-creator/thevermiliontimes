State Puts SpaceX’s Wharf and Road Permit Out for Public Comment. Here’s How to Weigh In.

Louisiana’s coastal permit office marked the application complete this week and sent it out for review. The 25-day comment window opens with a legal notice set to run Saturday.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 20, 2026, 6:20 a.m.

The first big state permit for SpaceX’s Vermilion Parish project is now in front of the public.

Louisiana’s coastal permit office has put SpaceX’s application for a Gulf wharf, barge docks and a heavy-haul road near Pecan Island “on notice,” according to the state’s online permit tracker. That starts the clock on public comment.

How to comment

The Advocate, which is publishing the state’s legal notice, reports that the notice runs Saturday, Nov. 21, and that written comments will be taken for 25 days from that date. By our count that runs through Dec. 16.

According to the notice as the newspaper describes it:

  • Send comments to the reviewer assigned to the case, Melissa Sherman, at the Office of Permitting and Compliance, P.O. Box 44487, Baton Rouge, LA 70804-4487, or by email at melissa.sherman2@la.gov.
  • Put the permit number on it: P20260831.
  • Include your full name and contact information.
  • You can suggest changes, object and say why, or ask for a public hearing.

Where the application stands

The state’s tracker lists the project as the “Southwest Louisiana Coastal Infrastructure Improvement Project” and shows this history:

  • Sept. 25: SpaceX’s application arrives.
  • Oct. 1: The state sends it back to the engineering firm that filed it as incomplete.
  • Nov. 9: A revised application comes in.
  • Nov. 17: The state marks it complete, assigns it to an analyst and sends it to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the State Land Office.
  • Nov. 18: It goes to The Advocate for the legal notice and to the Department of Wildlife and Fisheries, and the status changes to “On Notice.”
  • Nov. 19: The Corps responds, and the application goes to the Department of Environmental Quality.

The tracker lists the application’s status as “Received - Under Review.” It does not show a decision or a hearing date.

What SpaceX is asking to build

As the Times reported from the application last week, the plan covers a Gulf-side wharf below the Freshwater Bayou lock, inland barge docks, canal dredging and a heavy-haul road of about 15.75 miles with a 1.5-mile bridge, with work running from 2027 to 2032. [Our earlier report has the details.](/news/spacex-permit-coastal-infrastructure-improvement-project-freshwater-bayou/)

The Advocate reports that the revised application filed this month says the wharf would let SpaceX ship Louisiana-made parts and equipment to its operations in Texas and Florida. The Times has not yet obtained that revised filing.

The federal side is separate. The Corps of Engineers reviews the same application under its own rules.
