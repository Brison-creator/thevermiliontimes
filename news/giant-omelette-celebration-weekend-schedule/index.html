Giant Omelette Weekend: Abbeville’s Celebration Runs Saturday and Sunday, With the Skillet Lit Sunday Afternoon

The Confrérie d’Abbeville’s festival fills downtown Nov. 7-8 with music, crafts, an antique car show and a tractor egg-cracking contest. The omelette itself is cooked only on Sunday, starting around 1:30 p.m.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 5, 2026, 3:55 p.m. · Updated Nov. 6, 2026

Downtown Abbeville turns into one big breakfast this weekend. The Giant Omelette Celebration runs Saturday, Nov. 7, and Sunday, Nov. 8, according to the schedule posted by the Confrérie d’Abbeville, the group that puts on the festival.

One thing to know before you go: the omelette is cooked on Sunday afternoon only. Saturday is music, food, crafts and old machinery; the big skillet comes out on Sunday.

Saturday, Nov. 7

  • 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.: antique implement display (visitors vote for a favorite), arts and crafts show and food show
  • 9 to 10:30 a.m.: fiddlers and dancers from LeBlanc Elementary, plus Vermilion Parish’s Lache Pas middle school students
  • 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.: Kids World games and activities
  • 11 a.m. to 1:30 p.m.: music by Blake Miller & the Old Fashioned Aces
  • 1:30 p.m.: tractor “egg cracking” contest, adults only, with trophies
  • 2 to 5 p.m.: Hunter Courville and Cajun Fever
  • 5 p.m.: grounds close for the night

Sunday, Nov. 8

  • 6 a.m.: registration for the “Bikes, Brews & Omelettes Two” bike ride (run through LaTrail.org)
  • 9 a.m.: official Mass at St. Mary Magdalen Catholic Church
  • 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.: arts and crafts and food shows; antique cars and implements on view until 5 p.m.
  • 10 a.m. to 1 p.m.: Geno Delafose & French Rockin’ Boogie
  • 1 p.m.: the chefs and the eggs parade toward the skillet
  • 1:15 p.m.: the TABASCO Girls Dance Team on Concord Street, and LeBlanc Elementary French immersion students singing
  • 1:30 p.m.: Fa Tras, a local Cajun band
  • 1:30 to 4:30 p.m.: the omelette is made and served
  • 4:30 p.m.: festival ends

The procession is the signal that cooking is about to start. Confrérie members in chef’s dress walk through downtown, greeting the crowd, then lead the way down Concord Street to the 12-foot skillet, the festival says. French bread from Poupart’s Bakery in Lafayette travels with the procession and is sliced and served alongside the omelette.

The weather

It will feel like fall. The National Weather Service in Lake Charles is forecasting a partly sunny Saturday in Abbeville with a high near 74 and a north wind around 10 mph. Saturday night turns mostly cloudy with a low around 47, so bring a jacket for an early start Sunday. Sunday calls for partly sunny skies and a high near 68.

If you have questions

The Confrérie lists a general information line at 337-344-9232, and contacts for each event are on its website, giantomelette.org. Next year’s celebration is set for Nov. 6-7, 2027.
