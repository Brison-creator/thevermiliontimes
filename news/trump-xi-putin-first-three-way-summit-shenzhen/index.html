Trump, Xi and Putin Will Meet Together for the First Time Thursday in China

China’s Foreign Ministry confirmed the first-ever three-way summit of the U.S., Chinese and Russian leaders for Nov. 19 in Shenzhen. Washington says peace and denuclearization are on the table; Moscow says the agenda is still being written.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 16, 2026, 7:10 a.m.

The leaders of the world’s three biggest nuclear powers are about to sit at the same table for the first time.

China’s Foreign Ministry announced Monday that President Xi Jinping will host President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin for a China-Russia-U.S. summit on Thursday, Nov. 19, in Shenzhen, the southern Chinese tech city hosting this year’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meetings. Shenzhen is 14 hours ahead of Louisiana.

The three governments describe it as the first summit of its kind. The Kremlin calls it the “first-ever meeting in such a format.”

What each side says it wants

Washington: In a Nov. 11 call, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi talked about Trump’s trip to the summit in China and Xi’s planned visit to Florida for the G20. According to the State Department’s readout, the two agreed that leader-to-leader talks matter for goals “such as peace and denuclearization,” including at a possible three-way meeting with Russia.

Moscow: Putin’s foreign policy aide Yury Ushakov said both Russia and the U.S. “see great value” in the Shenzhen summit, but that work on what it will actually cover is still underway. “We hope that this first-ever meeting in such a format will have a stabilising effect on the overall international situation,” he said. Ushakov also said Putin and Trump plan their own meeting on the sidelines, where the war in Ukraine will come up.

Beijing: The Foreign Ministry’s announcement confirmed the date, the city and the three leaders, and little else.

Why it matters at home

Between them, the U.S., Russia and China hold the great majority of the world’s nuclear weapons. Any deal on arms, on Ukraine, or on trade between the three would reach far beyond Shenzhen, including the export markets Louisiana’s ports and farmers depend on.

The Times will report what comes out of Thursday’s meeting.
