SpaceX Is Leasing the Old Pecan Island School for 40 Years and Will Put at Least $10 Million Into It

UL Lafayette, which was given the building last year, announced the deal Wednesday. The agreement also allows for a sale. The university did not say what SpaceX will use it for.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 26, 2026, 8:10 a.m.

The old Pecan Island School is about to have a new tenant: SpaceX.

The University of Louisiana at Lafayette, which owns the building, announced Wednesday that it has agreed to lease the property to the rocket company for 40 years.

The deal

  • Length: A 40-year lease.
  • Investment: SpaceX will put at least $10 million into improvements to the former school, the university said.
  • A sale is possible. The university said the agreement “also contemplates a potential sale.”
  • How it is set up: The lease runs through Ragin’ Cajun Facilities Inc., and the University of Louisiana System’s Board of Supervisors approved it.

The announcement does not say what SpaceX plans to do with the building, how much rent it will pay, or when work will start.

The building

The school closed after Hurricane Rita in 2005. Three years later, brothers Peter M. John and David M. John bought it. They renovated it, kept the students’ original artwork and ran it as the Pecan Island School Lodge, opening it to former students, schools and community groups.

In 2025 the brothers gave the building to UL Lafayette for wildlife and coastal erosion research. The university describes it as 39,000 square feet and valued at about $1.4 million.

“Then came an unexpected opportunity,” the university’s announcement says: SpaceX’s plan to build Starbase Louisiana “just miles from the former school.”

What the brothers said

“When we donated the school to UL Lafayette, we wanted it to have a meaningful future,” Peter M. John said in the announcement. “It’s gratifying to know that a place once devoted to educating young people can continue to serve the pursuit of knowledge and discovery, while supporting new opportunities for this community and our state.”

“We never imagined that the school’s next chapter might be connected to something as ambitious as space exploration,” David M. John said.

The university said it will honor the family with a plaque on campus and a scholarship in their name.

What else the university is planning

UL Lafayette said it plans to start new programs in aerospace engineering, autonomous systems and space computing in the fall of 2027.

Why it matters here

A $10 million renovation is construction work in Pecan Island before the launch site itself is built. It also gives SpaceX a building in the middle of the community.

It comes the same week the state [put SpaceX’s coastal permit on hold](/news/state-puts-spacex-coastal-permit-on-hold-asks-missing-information/) and asked for more information.
