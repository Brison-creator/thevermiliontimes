Disagree All You Want. Threats of Violence Get Reported

A note from the publisher on the SpaceX fight, the comments on our page, and the line nobody gets to cross.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 28, 2026, 12:15 a.m.

By Brison McPhail, Publisher

This week, on our Facebook page, someone wrote that a man “should be buried in the swamp.” Someone else wrote that journalists “should be hung.”

That is not debate, and it will not stay on our page.

Here is our rule, starting today. If you post a comment calling for someone to be killed, hanged, shot or buried, we will save it, take it down, and send it and your profile to the Vermilion Parish Sheriff’s Office and Louisiana State Police. They can decide what is a crime. That is their job, not ours.

What Louisiana law says

We looked up the state laws. Here is what they say in plain words.

  • Cyberstalking (R.S. 14:40.3). It is a crime to send an electronic message that uses words “threatening to inflict bodily harm to any person.” A first conviction can bring a fine of up to $2,000, up to a year in jail, or both. The same law says it does not cover peaceful, nonthreatening speech about politics.
  • Threatening a public official or law enforcement officer (R.S. 14:122.2). This covers a serious threat of violence against a state official or an officer. The law names social media. The penalty is up to $500, up to six months in jail, or both. The law says the person “need not actually intend to carry out the threat.”
  • Terrorizing and menacing (R.S. 14:40.1). This covers telling the public a violent crime is about to happen in order to cause fear. Terrorizing can bring up to 15 years in prison.

We are not lawyers, and we are not the judge. Whether a comment breaks one of these laws is for police and prosecutors to decide. Our part is to send it to them.

The same rule for everyone

This goes for everybody. It does not matter if you love SpaceX or hate it. It does not matter who you voted for. A threat aimed at Elon Musk gets the same treatment as a threat aimed at a protester, a parish official or a neighbor.

A lot of the fight over SpaceX in Louisiana is not about SpaceX at all. It is about politics and about how people feel about one man. That fight is loud, and it drowns out the people with real questions.

And there are real questions. Why did the state put the coastal permit on hold? What does the tax deal give up, and what does the parish get back? What happens to the roads, the marsh, the wildlife and the seafood? Will the jobs go to people who live here?

Those questions deserve answers. We have been reporting on every one of them from the public record, and we will keep doing it. Ask them here. Argue about them here. Tell us when you think we got it wrong.

We are not outsiders to this. We own land in Vermilion Parish. What happens here happens to us too.

So disagree all you want. Be angry if you are angry. But nobody on this page gets to call for someone’s death.
