New Tariffs Proposed on 14 Trading Partners. Imported Shrimp and Rice Are Not on the Exemption Lists

Washington proposed tariffs of up to 25% on goods from India and up to 35% on goods from Vietnam. Nothing changes yet. Public comments are due Jan. 5.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 26, 2026, 3:55 p.m.

The federal government proposed new tariffs Wednesday on goods from 14 trading partners, including several countries that ship shrimp and fragrant rice to American stores.

A tariff is a tax paid when a product comes into the country. Nothing changes today. These are proposals, and the public has until Jan. 5 to weigh in.

For Vermilion Parish, the part to watch is what got left off the list of products that would be spared. The Times read the exemption lists for India, Vietnam, Thailand and Indonesia. No fish or shellfish is on them, and neither is rice. As written, imported shrimp and rice from those four countries would be covered.

What was proposed

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer announced the findings Nov. 25. His office said the 14 governments prop up their factories in ways that hurt American producers, and that the law known as Section 301 lets the United States answer with tariffs.

The proposed top rates:

  • Vietnam: up to 35%. That could drop to 20% if Vietnam signs a trade agreement with the United States.
  • India: up to 25%, or 17% with a trade agreement.
  • China: up to 20% on most goods, and up to 10% on a separate list of goods.
  • Singapore: up to 20%, or 10% with a trade agreement.
  • Thailand: up to 18%. The notice says that could go as high as 25% if talks with Thailand fall short.
  • Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia and Bangladesh: up to 18%.
  • The European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland: up to 15%.

Each number is a ceiling, not a final rate. The notices say the figures already count other Section 301 tariffs now in place, so the new tariff would not simply be stacked on top of those.

The trade office said it has not made a decision on Mexico or Norway, which were also under review.

What would be spared

The notices list products the new tariffs would skip. They include steel, aluminum and copper, cars and trucks and their parts, wood products, patented medicines and computer chips. Those already fall under separate tariffs.

Each notice also carries a long list of other exempt products, more than 2,000 tariff codes in the four notices we checked. Those lists include some beef, wheat seed, barley and oats. They do not include shrimp, crab, fish or rice.

Why that matters here

Shrimp boats work out of Delcambre and Intracoastal City, and rice is grown across the parish. Both compete with imports.

Frozen shrimp from India, Vietnam and Thailand is already under U.S. antidumping duty orders. Those are extra charges the Commerce Department puts on products it finds are sold here below fair price. The department finished its yearly review of each of the three orders between September and November.

On rice, the U.S. Department of Agriculture says the rice America imports is mostly fragrant rice from Asia: Thai jasmine, plus basmati grown in India and Pakistan.

A tariff makes an imported product cost more to bring in. Whether these would help local boats and farmers depends on the final rates and the final exemption lists, and neither is set. Products can be added to or taken off the lists after the comment period.

How to comment

  • Written comments are due Jan. 5, 2027. They go through the trade office’s portal at comments.ustr.gov. Each country has its own docket. The docket for written comments on India is USTR-2026-0731.
  • Requests to speak at the hearing are also due Jan. 5.
  • Hearings begin Jan. 25, 2027, at 10 a.m. at the U.S. International Trade Commission, 500 E Street SW, Washington.

The trade office asked for comments on which products should be covered or left out, and on what the rates should be.

The Times will report the final rates when they are published.
