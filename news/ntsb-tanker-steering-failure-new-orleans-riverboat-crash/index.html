Tanker’s Rudder Jammed Two Minutes Before It Rammed a Docked Riverboat in New Orleans, NTSB Says

Federal investigators say the Atlantic Asphalt’s steering failed as it passed under the Crescent City Connection Friday, sending it into the American Heritage. Everyone aboard the riverboat was accounted for.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 15, 2026, 4:30 p.m.

The tanker that slammed into a docked riverboat in New Orleans on Friday lost its steering about two minutes before the crash, federal investigators said Sunday.

The Atlantic Asphalt, an asphalt tanker heading upriver, had just passed under the Crescent City Connection at about 12:26 p.m. on Nov. 13 when it suffered what the National Transportation Safety Board called a “steering casualty.” Its rudder was stuck turned to starboard, and the ship swung toward the river’s east bank, NTSB investigator-in-charge Bart Barnum said at a briefing.

At about 12:28 p.m., it hit the American Heritage, an American Cruise Lines paddlewheel riverboat tied up near the Port of New Orleans. Everyone aboard the riverboat was accounted for.

Two minutes on the recorder

Investigators have more than nine hours of recordings from the ship’s “black box,” its voyage data recorder, which logs bridge audio, rudder angle, engine data, speed and heading, Barnum said. In those final two minutes, the audio picks up the ship’s whistle, radio warnings and calls for help, orders to drop the anchors and emergency maneuvering commands.

Investigators don’t yet know why the steering failed. They began interviewing the riverboat’s crew Sunday and plan to talk to the tanker’s crew starting Monday.

“We are investigating the personnel involved, the ships involved, and the environment at the time,” NTSB board member John DeLeeuw said. He said investigators will also study “the things that went right,” since no one was killed.

What’s next

The NTSB expects a preliminary report in about 30 days and wants to hear from anyone who saw the crash or caught it on camera.

The crash matters well beyond New Orleans. The lower Mississippi is one of the busiest shipping lanes in the country, and traffic on that stretch of the river was halted after the collision.
