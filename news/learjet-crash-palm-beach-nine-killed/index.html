Nine Killed as Business Jet From Austin Crashes Into Palm Beach County Neighborhood

Updated Monday: The NTSB says there is evidence the Learjet began breaking up before it hit the ground, minutes after the crew reported ice and turbulence at 35,000 feet.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 1, 2026, 11:18 p.m. · Updated Nov. 2, 2026

Update, Monday: what the NTSB said

Tom Chapman, a member of the National Transportation Safety Board, told reporters in West Palm Beach on Monday that investigators have “some evidence that there was some breakup of the aircraft before impact,” as reported by WPEC and The Associated Press. He said parts of the jet may turn up some distance from where it hit.

Chapman laid out the final minutes, based on the crew’s radio calls, according to WPEC:

  • The jet left Austin around 10:37 a.m. with two crew members and seven passengers.
  • About 12:47 p.m., cruising at 35,000 feet, the pilots radioed that they were picking up a light coating of clear ice, riding through moderate turbulence and drifting about 700 feet off their altitude in air that was 47 below zero Celsius. They said the ice was under control.
  • The crew then reported precipitation and asked to climb to 37,000 feet, which controllers approved.
  • About 12:48 p.m., the jet radioed that it was in an “overspeed condition descending.” The rest of the transmission could not be understood.

Chapman said the board is looking at whether weather played a role but would not speculate on a cause this early, WPEC reported. Investigator Timothy Monville put the wreckage area at about 2,600 feet long and about 2,100 feet wide, spread over ball fields, businesses and homes.

Monville also said a man driving out of the area suffered a leg injury, the first report of anyone hurt on the ground. County Commissioner Joel Flores said about 77 mobile homes were evacuated and gave no timeline for residents to return, according to the AP.

Authorities have not yet identified the nine victims publicly. The NTSB asks witnesses to email witness@ntsb.gov and asks anyone who finds debris to leave it where it is.

The first hours

A business jet carrying nine people slammed into a Palm Beach County, Fla., neighborhood Sunday, killing everyone on board, U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

“All 9 people on board were killed,” Duffy wrote in a post on X.

The plane, a Learjet 60, went down around 12:50 p.m. local time near Cresthaven, according to a statement from the Federal Aviation Administration. The jet left Austin-Bergstrom International Airport headed for North Eleuthera, an island airport in the Bahamas. The FAA said the information is preliminary.

On the ground

The jet came down along Military Trail near Greenacres, leaving a crater in the road, Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis said after visiting the scene. “Obviously, this was a cataclysmic plane crash,” DeSantis said.

Palm Beach County officials said that as of their news conference they had no reports of anyone on the ground being killed or hurt. They said about 600 people living in a nearby mobile home community were expected to be moved out while investigators work, with two shelters ready and county buses on standby, WPTV reported.

The county closed Military Trail from 10th Avenue North up to Purdy Lane and Alameda Drive until further notice.

The investigation

The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation, with the FAA’s help. NTSB investigators were arriving at the site Sunday and Monday morning, and the board scheduled a Monday news conference in West Palm Beach, according to the NTSB.

Officials have not said what caused the crash or released the names of the people on board. Recordings and accounts of the pilot’s final radio calls are circulating online; The Vermilion Times has not verified them and will rely on the NTSB’s findings.

The FAA usually posts a preliminary report the next business day. The NTSB’s preliminary report typically follows within weeks, and a final cause can take a year or more.
