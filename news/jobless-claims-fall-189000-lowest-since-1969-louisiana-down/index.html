New Unemployment Claims Fall to 189,000, Matching the Lowest Level Since 1969. Louisiana’s Are Down 29% From Last Year

Fewer Americans filed for unemployment last week than in almost any week since 1969. In Louisiana, about 1,150 people a week are filing, down from more than 1,600 a year ago.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 25, 2026, 12:35 p.m.

The number of Americans losing their jobs and filing for unemployment fell last week to one of the lowest levels in more than half a century.

About 189,000 people filed new claims in the week that ended Nov. 21, according to the Labor Department’s weekly count, released a day early because of Thanksgiving. That is down 13,000 from the week before.

The count has been this low only one other time since September 1969: in July of this year, when it was also 189,000.

The national numbers

  • New claims last week: 189,000. A year ago the same week it was 218,000.
  • Four-week average: 196,000. That smooths out the bumps from one week to the next.
  • People still drawing benefits: about 1.7 million, as of the week that ended Nov. 14.

New claims are the closest thing there is to a weekly count of layoffs. A low number means employers are holding on to the workers they have.

Louisiana

The state numbers run a week behind and are not adjusted for the season, so the fair comparison is with the same weeks last year.

  • New claims in Louisiana: 1,143 in the week that ended Nov. 14.
  • Over the past four weeks: about 1,150 a week on average. In the same four weeks last year it was about 1,630. That is a drop of 29%.
  • Louisianans still drawing benefits: 5,023 in the week that ended Nov. 7, down from 7,858 a year earlier, a drop of 36%.

The same direction

Louisiana has added 17,600 construction jobs in the past year, faster growth than any other state. [We reported that Sunday.](/news/louisiana-adding-construction-jobs-faster-than-any-state/) Both numbers point the same way: more hiring, fewer layoffs.

A caution

The newest weekly figures are first counts, and they get revised. Weeks around holidays are harder to adjust and can jump around.
