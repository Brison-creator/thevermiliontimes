Jensen Huang Gets His Job Back: Nvidia’s Board Reinstates Its CEO 11 Days After He Resigned

The board says it heard from “senior levels” of the U.S. government and is confident his return is no national security risk. An investigation into a foreign intelligence effort aimed at him is still open.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 23, 2026, 11:05 p.m.

Jensen Huang is running Nvidia again.

The company’s board voted unanimously Monday to put him back as president and chief executive and to return him to his board seat, effective immediately. He had resigned from all three on Nov. 12.

Nvidia makes the computer chips behind much of the artificial intelligence boom. Huang co-founded the company and led it for 33 years.

What the board said

The board said it reached the decision after “a series of discussions and meetings” with Huang and was “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead.”

It also said this: the board “received input from senior levels of the United States government,” and based on that, it is “confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

“I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” Huang said in the company’s announcement. “I can’t wait to get back to work.”

How it got here

All of this is from Nvidia’s own announcements:

  • Nov. 8: The board opens an independent investigation “in light of recent reporting” that Huang “has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.” It says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.
  • Nov. 10: The independent directors hire a former acting U.S. attorney, Joon H. Kim, and his law firm to run the investigation.
  • Nov. 12: Huang resigns. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress becomes interim CEO, and the board says it will search for a permanent replacement.
  • Nov. 17: The board creates a committee to oversee national security and export rules.
  • Nov. 23: The board reinstates Huang.

Nvidia’s statements do not describe the intelligence effort. NBC News, citing earlier reports by The New York Times and the Financial Times, said the reporting concerned Huang’s relationship with a woman whom American intelligence officials suspected of spying for China.

The president’s part

Nvidia did not say who in the government it heard from.

NBC News reported that President Donald Trump said in a television interview that he personally stepped in, speaking “directly and indirectly” with the board. NBC also reported that Trump wrote on social media that the government had “no reason to believe” Huang’s return poses a national security risk, and that the attorney general had confirmed the government has no evidence Huang did anything unlawful.

Not everyone accepted that. NBC quoted Sen. John Cornyn, a Texas Republican on the Senate Intelligence Committee, as writing: “No national security risk? Based on what?”

Nvidia’s stock rose 31% Monday, NBC reported.

What is not over

  • The investigation continues. The board said the independent committee’s work “will proceed to conclusion” and that Huang supports it and will cooperate.
  • New watchdogs. Nvidia is hiring a chief compliance officer, a new position, and the board’s national security committee is bringing in outside experts.
  • Congress. NBC reported that Huang is still scheduled for a closed-door interview with three House committees on Dec. 1 and a public Senate hearing later that week.
  • Government inquiries. Nvidia said it “will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.”

Kress goes back to being chief financial officer. Harvey Jones stays on as board chair.
