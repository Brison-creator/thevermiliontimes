Citizen Group Presses for Answers on SpaceX Deal; Ex-Landry Aide Now a SpaceX Lobbyist

Vermilion United says the Starbase Louisiana tax agreement treats local hiring as a goal, not a promise, and wants a verbal SpaceX pledge on fire protection put in writing.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Oct. 31, 2026, 5:48 a.m.

A month-old Vermilion Parish citizens’ group says the paperwork behind Starbase Louisiana leaves residents with more hopes than guarantees, and it wants answers before construction starts.

Vermilion United came together days after Gov. Jeff Landry unveiled the SpaceX deal, organizer Melissa Hargrave told Acadiana News First in a report published Thursday. Since then the group has gotten hold of recordings from two meetings of the parish Industrial Development Board, held July 23 and Aug. 25, and it now posts transcripts online next to the text of the signed tax agreement so residents can compare what was said with what was signed.

“Our goal as a coalition is to make sure that we get as much information as possible so we can inform the public,” Hargrave told the station.

Three questions the group is pressing

  • Local jobs. The payment-in-lieu-of-taxes agreement sets hiring targets, but the group says local hiring is written as a goal, not an obligation, so there is no promise the jobs go to parish residents.
  • A $600,000 fee. The development board’s yearly administrative fee, capped at $600,000, is slated to go to One Acadiana, a privately run organization that promotes the wider Acadiana region. Hargrave argues the parish should keep it to restart its own economic development district. The station could not confirm whether that arrangement was ever approved, and One Acadiana was not available for an interview.
  • Fire protection. In the recordings, the board’s lawyer says SpaceX verbally promised to supply firefighting equipment if the project lowers the parish’s fire rating, which could raise homeowners’ insurance. Hargrave wants that promise “codified in writing.” It is not in the signed tax agreement; it could be in the state’s separate cooperative endeavor agreement with SpaceX, which has not been made public. The group has requested that document from Louisiana Economic Development and was told it would come no sooner than Dec. 14.

A former Landry aide lobbies for SpaceX

Meanwhile, The Center Square reported that Kyle Ruckert, Landry’s chief of staff until February, is now registered to lobby for SpaceX, in a pay bracket of $100,000 to $249,000. Ruckert, who chairs the Landry-aligned nonprofit Protect Louisiana Values, could not be reached, the outlet said.

The same report puts the project’s promise at 3,000 direct jobs within ten years, paying $92,600 on average, with work starting in 2027 and a first launch aimed for 2029.

The citizens’ group is not the only challenge: three residents and the Vermilion Citizens Coalition have also sued to void the tax deal. The Times reported on that suit Friday.
