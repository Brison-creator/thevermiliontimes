New Order Tells Every Federal Contractor to Alert the White House Before Answering Congress

Companies that work for the federal government, and their subcontractors, must report requests from Congress and follow White House instructions on what to hold back. Breaking the rule could cost a company its contracts.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 22, 2026, 4:20 p.m.

President Donald Trump signed an order dated Friday that puts a new rule on every company that does work for the federal government.

If Congress asks a contractor about its work or its communications with the executive branch, the company must tell the White House and the agency it works for right away. It must then follow any instructions it gets about holding back material the White House or the agency decides is covered by executive privilege.

The order is Executive Order 14438. It says contractors must do this “immediately.”

Who it covers

  • Every federal contractor. The order does not limit the rule to defense or security work. A company that builds, hauls, supplies or does engineering under a federal contract is covered.
  • Subcontractors too. The order says it applies to subcontractors working on a federal contract, and it makes the main contractor responsible for seeing that they follow it.
  • Requests already pending, and any that come later.

What happens if a company doesn’t

Agencies are told to consider “all appropriate actions,” and the order names three: ending or canceling the company’s contracts, suspending the company, or barring it from future federal work.

Why the White House says it is needed

The order says the executive branch has to be able to share sensitive information with contractors without it being passed on, “including to members of the Congress.” It says some congressional leaders have threatened to seek confidential information and have been “harassing private companies that contract with the Federal Government.”

It also argues that when a contractor and the government answer the same request separately, the result is wasted effort and money.

What it does not settle

The order says it is to be carried out “consistent with applicable law.” It does not say how it fits with a company’s legal duty to answer a subpoena from Congress, and the text does not mention subpoenas. An executive order does not need a vote in Congress.

The White House budget office is told to write guidance for agencies on how to check that contractors are complying. Agencies are told to notify their contractors and subcontractors promptly.
