We Asked Vermilion Parish’s Three Power Providers How They Guard the Grid From Cyberattacks. One Answered.

Entergy sent a short statement. The City of Abbeville said it couldn’t verify an answer in time. SLEMCO did not respond.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 20, 2026, 9:40 a.m.

After a week in which flights were grounded nationwide and bank apps and Apple’s App Store went down, we had a simple question: what about the lights?

So the Times asked the three organizations that deliver electricity in Vermilion Parish, Entergy Louisiana, the City of Abbeville and SLEMCO, how they protect their systems from cyberattacks, including attacks helped along by artificial intelligence. We sent written questions Monday and Tuesday, followed up Thursday, and set a final deadline of 9 a.m. Friday.

We told each one we were not asking for technical details that could help an attacker.

Entergy: a statement

Entergy answered Thursday with a statement emailed by Brandon Scardigli:

“Entergy has dedicated cyber and physical security teams, a critical infrastructure protection department and a nuclear cybersecurity team responsible for implementing, maintaining and monitoring regulatory requirements. We maintain our information security status through ongoing diligence, investment and continuous monitoring.”

The statement did not address the rest of what we asked: whether attempted attacks are increasing, whether the company uses AI tools on defense, how it tests changes before they go live, or what customers in Vermilion Parish should know.

City of Abbeville: not in time

Abbeville runs its own electric system. Cristian LeJeune, the city’s media and communications coordinator, wrote Thursday that he was not comfortable speaking about the system’s cybersecurity without first confirming the information was accurate, and that the staff who could confirm it had been tied up with other pressing matters.

“I do not anticipate being able to provide a verified statement in time for publication,” he wrote.

We told the city we’ll add its statement to this story when it is ready.

SLEMCO: no response

SLEMCO, the member-owned electric cooperative, did not respond by the deadline. An automatic reply from its communications director said she was out of the office until Nov. 23. We sent the request to the alternate contact named in that reply on Tuesday and followed up Thursday, and did not hear back.

What we asked

Each provider got the same basic questions:

  • How do you protect the system from cyberattacks, including AI-assisted ones?
  • Are attempted attacks increasing?
  • Do you use AI tools yourselves to defend the system?
  • How do you test software and equipment changes before they go live?
  • Who oversees your cybersecurity?
  • What should customers watch for, including scams?

What Washington is saying

The outages the White House addressed this week involved the FAA, banks and Microsoft, and officials said none of them was an attack. In a release Wednesday, the White House said it sees no sign of a coordinated cyberattack or a foreign government behind them. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton put the trouble down to a wave of repairs: organizations are taking systems down to patch and upgrade them, and “they’re not coming back as expected in all cases.”

“Our systems need upgrades. They need patching,” Clayton said. [Our report on that is here.](/news/white-house-outages-not-linked-not-cyberattack/)

We’ll keep asking, and we’ll update this story as answers come in.
