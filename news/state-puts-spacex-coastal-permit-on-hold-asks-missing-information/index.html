State Puts SpaceX’s Pecan Island Permit on Hold and Asks for Pages of Missing Information

The coastal permit for the wharf, canal and haul road went on hold Monday, five days after it went out for public comment. The state’s top regulator says the review has not stopped.

By The Vermilion Times · Published by Brison McPhail
Nov. 26, 2026, 7:05 a.m.

The state permit SpaceX needs before it can dredge, build wharves or lay a haul road at Pecan Island is on hold.

The state’s online permit tracker shows the application’s status as “Received - On Hold.” The entry that put it there is dated Monday, Nov. 23, at 11:37 a.m.: “On-Hold Pending Response by Submitter.”

The submitter is Duplantis Design Group, the Lafayette engineering firm that filed the application for SpaceX.

What the state told SpaceX

The state sent a letter the same day. WBRZ and KATC both reported on it, and KATC published it.

“After careful review of your application for a Coastal Use Permit, we have determined that we are unable to continue the processing of the application until we receive the following information,” the letter says, according to both stations.

What follows is a list several pages long. As WBRZ described it, the state wants:

  • Proof of coordination with the state wildlife agency and the state’s coastal restoration agency, to protect wetlands and existing shoreline projects nearby.
  • More on environmental damage and repair: an updated table of habitat impacts, a look at the areas where dredged material would be drained and piped, how canal banks would be stabilized, and a plan to put temporary construction areas back the way they were.
  • Fixed engineering drawings for the canal dredging, the inland wharf, the Gulf wharf and the roadway, with missing cross sections and dimensions added and conflicts in elevations, acreage totals and maps resolved.

A second letter, to the Army Corps

KATC reported that the state also wrote to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, which has to issue its own federal permit.

“Due to lack of necessary information, this office has determined that the proposed activity is inconsistent with the Louisiana Coastal Resources Program,” that letter says, according to KATC. “Therefore, please do not issue a Section 404 Permit unless or until this inconsistency is resolved.”

The letter gives six months. If the problems are not fixed by then, the state says it will tell the Corps the project is inconsistent with Louisiana’s coastal program.

“It’s not paused,” the secretary says

Dustin Davidson, the secretary of the Department of Conservation and Energy, told WBRZ that calling the review paused is wrong.

“The fact that we’ve sent them questions indicates to you that we are actively reviewing it,” he said.

He also said most applicants do not get an application completely right the first time, whether it is a project this size or a fishing pier.

How it got here

From the state’s tracker:

  • Sept. 25: Application first received.
  • Oct. 1: Returned to the submitter as incomplete.
  • Nov. 9: Received again.
  • Nov. 17: Declared complete and assigned to an analyst.
  • Nov. 18: Put on public notice.
  • Nov. 23: Put on hold pending a response.

What it means

  • Nothing can be built under this permit until it is issued. SpaceX has said construction starts in 2027.
  • The clock is SpaceX’s. The state says it will pick the review back up when the information arrives.
  • Public comment. The state’s tracker still offers a form for comments on the application. The state has not said whether the hold changes the comment period that opened last week. [Our earlier story explains the project and how to comment.](/news/spacex-wharf-road-permit-public-comment-opens/)

We have not seen a public response from SpaceX. KATC said it asked Duplantis Design Group and the governor’s office for comment.
